Built around OGRA’s rulebook, from day one.

The LPG sector in Pakistan is regulated by the Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority under the LPG (Production and Distribution) Rules, 2001. MK Energies is structuring its operations, infrastructure and documentation to meet these requirements as it establishes and grows.

Compliance pathway

Five checkpoints between incorporation and supply.

From company registration through to an authorized distributor network, each checkpoint is built into how we set up the business.

A note on licensing. MK Energies (Private) Limited is a newly formed company. Licensing, storage-plant approvals and distributor authorizations are pursued and maintained through OGRA’s formal application process. This page describes our compliance commitment, not a claim of licenses already granted.

  1. 01
    SECP incorporation

    Registered as a private limited company under Pakistani corporate law, the foundation OGRA requires before any licensing step.

  2. 02
    OGRA marketing license

    Application and site documentation prepared in line with OGRA’s checklist for LPG marketing licenses.

  3. 03
    Storage & filling plant standards

    Site selection, distance and zoning requirements, and third-party inspection built into facility planning.

  4. 04
    Transport registration

    Road bowsers operated only through OGRA-registered vehicles and trained drivers.

  5. 05
    Authorized distributor network

    Dealers and distributors onboarded under formal agreements consistent with OGRA’s distribution framework.

For customers

Using LPG safely at home and at work.

LPG is safe when it is stored and used correctly. These are the basics we ask every customer and dealer to follow.

Everyday safety

  • Keep cylinders upright, in a well-ventilated place, away from heat and open flames.
  • Check the regulator and hose regularly, and replace any hose that is cracked, hard or worn.
  • Close the regulator or cylinder valve when the appliance is not in use.
  • Buy only from authorized dealers, and never accept a cylinder with a damaged valve or broken seal.
  • LPG is heavier than air, so leaked gas collects near the floor and in low spaces.

If you smell gas

  1. Don’t touch electrical switches or plugs, and don’t use a phone near the leak.
  2. Put out any flames and don’t smoke.
  3. Close the cylinder valve or regulator if you can do so safely.
  4. Open doors and windows to let the gas disperse, especially at floor level.
  5. Leave the area and call your LPG supplier, or Rescue 1122, from a safe distance.

LPG has an added odour so that leaks can be noticed. If the smell is strong, leave first and call for help.
